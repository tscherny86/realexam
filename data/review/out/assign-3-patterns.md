# Review batch 3 – systematic problems (s05e01–s05e10, s06e01–s06e10, x2025-09-22, x2025-11-18, x2025-11-21, x2025-12-16, x2026-06-24)

25 episodes, 64 MAIN assignments removed (see assign-3.tsv). No MINOR assignment was absurd enough to remove.

## 1. Search words that produce false MAIN hits

### Language points that fire on ordinary speech, not on Trevor explaining anything
- **Reported Speech – `he said that` / `she told me`**: Trevor summarises every speaker with "he said that …". Fires in s05e01, s06e04, s06e05 with zero reported-speech teaching. Suggest keeping only `reported speech`, `indirect speech`, `reporting verbs?`.
- **Slang & Informal English – `gonna`, `wanna`**: Trevor says "gonna" in every intro/outro, so the pattern alone reaches the threshold (s05e01, s05e08, x2025-11-21; also the show-notes evidence is usually just "you're gonna hear"). Drop `gonna`/`wanna`; keep `slang`, `informal`, `colloquial` and the lexical items (`grand`, `mate`, `bloke`, `dodgy`).
- **Connectors & Linking Words – `however`, `whereas`**: speakers use these constantly (s06e03, x2025-12-16). Only count them when Trevor also says "connector/linking/discourse marker/cohesi…" in the episode.
- **Conditionals – `unless`, `if I were`**: ordinary speech (s06e03). Keep only `conditional` and the inverted forms `had I`, `were I`.
- **Past Perfect & Narrative Tenses – `used to`**: nearly every speaker says "used to be" (s05e02, s06e07). Also `past simple` fires on "past simple after I wish" (s05e10), which is not a narrative-tense point. Keep `past perfect`, `narrative tense`, `pluperfect`, `past continuous`; drop `used to` and `past simple` or require them together with "past perfect".
- **Prepositions – `in the end`, `interested in`, `at the end`**: fire on speakers (s05e04) and on Trevor explaining the *expression* "at the end of the day" (s06e04). Keep only `preposition` / `dependent preposition`.
- **Collocations – `go with`**: matches "go with the flow", "go with your gut", "go with that option" (x2025-12-16, s06e10). Drop `\bgo with\b`; `goes together with` is fine.
- **Organising Your Answer – `organi[sz]…`, `firstly`**: "organization and prioritization go hand in hand" (s06e08, a speaker on time management) and Trevor's own "Firstly, he said…" (s06e10). Require `structure (your|an) answer`, `well organi[sz]ed`, `organi[sz]ation of (the|her|his|this) answer` or a show-notes hit.
- **Common Mistakes – `mistake`**: fires on "making mistakes" as a learner attitude (fine in x2025-11-21/x2025-12-16, but not in x2026-06-24 where it is "if you make a mistake in the exam, move on") and on the make/do quiz item "A mistake – make" (s05e10). Require `common mistake`, `error`, `incorrect`, `people (often|always) say`.
- **Idioms & Expressions – `expression`**: "a fancy expression" in exam-interaction advice (x2026-06-24, 10 hits, no idiom taught). Low priority – it is right almost everywhere else.
- **Spelling – `spelt`**: fires on the homophone explanation "spelt differently but sound the same" (s06e06); that episode should be Confusing Words & Homophones, which only got MINOR despite "a lovely homophone" in the show notes.
- **Pronunciation – `sounds? like`, and the app advert**: "feedback about your pronunciation" from the italki/app advert plus "made it sound like he said" (s06e01). Exclude the advert block (it repeats from s06e01 on) and drop `sounds? like`.
- **Speaking Exam – `examiner`**: fires on Trevor's cleft example "It is the examiner that you need to impress" (s05e09) and on "impress examiners" asides. Fine when combined with `speaking (exam|test)`.
- **Cambridge C1/C2 – `\bC1\b`, `\bC2\b`**: Trevor's class advert ("groups for B2, C1 and C2 levels") and "great for C1 or C2 exams" asides push these to MAIN (s05e08, s06e02). Exclude the advert block and require ≥ 1 non-advert mention plus something like "C1 writing", "C1 exam" discussed.

### Topics whose key word has another sense
- **Change & Life Changes – `change`**: "climate change" (s05e09 got MAIN with "title" evidence). Add a negative look-behind for `climate `.
- **The Environment & Climate Change – `environment`**: "positive learning environment" (s05e04, 8 hits). Use `the environment`, `environmental`, `natural environment` or exclude `learning environment`.
- **Communication – `chat`**: "we have a chat about…" (x2025-12-16). Drop `\bchat\b` or require `online chat`, `group chat`, `chat with friends`.
- **News & Media – title `News - …`**: the bonus/news episodes are titled "News - I made an app!" so the title match fires (x2025-11-18). Ignore the title prefix before " - " for x-episodes.
- **Learning, School & Education – `student`, `teacher`, `study`, `class`**: Trevor's own "my students", "English teacher", "classes", "study the language in the transcript" and the italki advert produce 10–12 hits in episodes with no education content (s05e03, s05e05, s05e06, s06e02, s06e10). Suggest excluding Trevor's advert/outro blocks and not counting "my students / as an English teacher".
- **Home & Housing – `home`, `neighbour`, `house`**: "at home", "come home from work", "buying a house" as an example, "neighbours helping" (s06e07, s06e10). `home` alone is too broad; `chores`/`housework` is what made s06e09 right.
- **Parenting & Children – `kids`, `child`**: "kids" as pupils in the School episode (s05e04, where "parent" never occurs), kids as recipients of help (s06e07), personal anecdotes (s06e05). Consider requiring `parent`/`parenting`/`upbringing`/`raise` or a children-related exam question.
- **Work & Careers – `work`**: "the work of Edward Hall", "work on together", "police work", "it works" (s06e07; s06e03 is borderline). Already min 6; still passes on the verb sense.
- **Help & Kindness – `help`**: "help out at home", app advert "if you need extra help" (x2025-11-18, s06e09).
- **Motivation & Goals – `goals`, `achiev`**: app advert "your language goals / achieving your goals" (x2025-11-18).
- **Shopping & Consumerism – `shops`**: shops closing down as a feature of a hometown (s05e07), not shopping.
- **Humour & Comedy – `funny`**: "a funny conversation", "they crack me up", and "Funny – hilarious" in the extreme-adjective quiz (s05e03).
- **Health & Wellbeing – `sleep`**: "drop off to sleep" as a phrasal-verb question (s05e05).
- **Languages & Language Learning – `language`**: "language relating to towns", "time for a language break", "new language" (s05e07, s06e04). `language break` and `new language` should be excluded; `learn(ing)? English`, `bilingual`, `mother tongue` are the reliable ones.
- **Friends & Friendship – `friends`**: friends as companions for weekend activities (s05e10, 14 hits) without any friendship discussion.

### Accents – country names as examples, not speakers
- **Non-Native Speakers – `Spain`, `Spanish`, `French`, `Italian`, `Vietnam`**: Trevor lives in Spain and uses "I should study more Spanish", "here in Spain", "Spanish listeners", "façade comes from French" as examples; a native Australian speaker lives in Vietnam. Six episodes got MAIN with only native speakers (s05e02, s05e05, s05e06, s05e07, x2025-09-22, s06e06). Country words should only count with `non-native`, `student of mine`, `learner`, `interview with`, or a title/show-notes hit.
- **USA – `US`, `American`, `North America`**: "the US is couch country", "Australian and US adjective", "American dramas" without a US speaker (s05e03, x2025-12-16; s05e06 is borderline – Trevor explains a southern-US phrase).
- **Australia / New Zealand** as destinations: "move to Australia" in a story (s06e10), "flew over to New Zealand" (s05e10).
- The show-notes speaker lists are reliable ("speakers from Canada, Australia, South Africa, England and Wales") – an accent named there with a speaker present should be MAIN regardless of transcript count, and one not named there should need a transcript sentence like "speaker from X" to become MAIN.

## 3. Logic observations

- **Trevor's recurring advert/outro blocks** (classes, transcripts, the app from s06e01 on, the italki code) inflate Learning/Education, Technology, Pronunciation, Help, Motivation, C1/C2 and Languages. Stripping the standard advert paragraphs before counting would remove a large share of the false MAINs in this batch.
- **Title evidence for x-episodes** uses the whole title including the category prefix ("News - …", "Monologue - …", "Bonus - …"). Only the part after " - " should count.
- **Show-notes evidence is strong for Language points and Accents but weak for Topics**: "learning phrasal verbs", "language relating to towns", "a funny conversation", "between cities and the countryside" are phrasing, not subjects. A show-notes hit should perhaps promote to MAIN only for Language/Exams/Accents, and for Topics only when the transcript also passes the threshold.
- **Language points are MAIN whenever a pattern passes the threshold, even if only speakers use the structure.** A cheap improvement: for Language entries require at least one hit in a Trevor paragraph (the commentary paragraphs, not the answers), since the point only exists as a "feature of the episode" when Trevor talks about it.
- **Topic thresholds**: Learning/Education (min 6) and Work (min 6) still pass on incidental uses; Non-Native Speakers (accent min 2) is far too low given how often Trevor mentions Spain.
- The interview episodes (x2025-11-21, x2025-12-16) are tagged well on the whole; the problems there are the generic ones above (`chat`, `go with`, however/whereas).
